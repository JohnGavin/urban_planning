Ranking Test Information

Source: Info_Raumplanung_Info_AV_RPL_TU_2026.pdf — Created by the Centre for Strategic Teaching Development at TU Wien, February 2026

Important: The actual exam is conducted entirely in German. This English translation is provided to help with understanding the content and structure. You must be able to read and answer all questions in German on exam day.

Test Structure and Scoring

The written ranking test comprises three parts:

PartContentWeight
Part ASubject knowledge from the prescribed study material (Subject Section)40%
Part BText comprehension20%
Part CCognitive abilities40%

The exam lasts exactly two hours. The test is designed so that all parts can be fully completed within this time.

The points achieved are weighted (recalculated so that each test part corresponds to its assigned weight). The weighted values of the test parts are summed to produce the overall result, which determines your ranking position.

Tips:
  • You can choose the order in which you work through the test parts.
  • Your answers are evaluated electronically — work precisely and neatly.
  • Only answers on the answer sheet are valid.

Part A: Subject Knowledge (40%)

This part tests your learning ability. You must answer multiple-choice questions that refer to the prescribed study material.

  • Each question has several answer options
  • One or more (but never all) answers can be correct
  • You receive partial credit for each correct answer
  • You achieve full marks when you tick all correct answers
  • Warning: Wrong answers are deducted from correct ones
  • You cannot score less than zero points per question

Study Material for Part A

The subject section is based on two documents:

  1. Austrian Spatial Development Concept OEREK 2030 (178 pages) — Knowledge Base
  2. Curriculum for the Bachelor's Programme in Spatial Planning (66 pages) — Knowledge Base

From the curriculum, the following are particularly relevant: the qualification profile, duration and scope, and the structure of the programme. A basic understanding of the contents and an overview of course content is tested. The exact titles of individual examination subjects, modules, and courses are not relevant for the exam.

Example Question

Question: Which tasks are part of the professional profile of spatial planners?

a) Analysis of the physical properties of rooms/spaces
b) Preparation of legally compliant building plans
c) Design of planning processes
d) Development of concepts for interior design
e) Preparation of structural engineering reports
Answer: a, c — Spatial planners analyse space (physical, geographical, social) and design planning processes. Building plans, interior design, and structural reports belong to other professions (architects, interior designers, structural engineers). Note: option a) uses "Räumen" which in the spatial planning context means geographic/social spaces, not rooms.

Part B: Text Comprehension (20%)

This part tests your ability to absorb, understand, and correctly contextualise the content of texts.

  • You receive texts in German together with statements referring to these texts
  • For each statement, decide: true, false, or cannot be determined
  • Base your answer only on the text, not on your prior knowledge

Example: Town Centre Revitalisation in Silz

The municipality of Silz in Tyrol was confronted several years ago with increasing desolation of its town centre. The deterioration of buildings, some with historic building fabric up to 300 years old, greatly reduced the quality of living in the centre of the municipality. A major challenge was also to counteract the vacancy of houses. The decision was made to implement revitalisation measures, as the alternative — rezoning of areas at the outer edges of the municipality — would have brought the negative effects of urban sprawl and soil sealing. Over the past 14 years, 83 buildings in Silz have been renovated. The investment volume amounted to 12 million euros, of which the province and the municipality co-financed 700,000 euros.

Source: ÖROK: 15th Spatial Planning Report, ÖROK Publication Series No. 204/2018

1. Unused buildings can contribute to urban sprawl.

→ Cannot be determined (c) — The text states that rezoning leads to sprawl, not vacant buildings directly.

2. The majority of investment funds were provided by the municipality.

→ False (b) — 12 million euros total investment, of which only 700,000 came from the province and municipality. The majority (11.3 million) came from private funds.

3. Excursions offered residents an attractive opportunity for participation.

→ Cannot be determined (c) — The text mentions excursions as part of citizen participation, but does not say whether they were "attractive".

Part C: Cognitive Abilities (40%)

This part contains different task types testing cognitive abilities:

1. Analogies (Analogical Reasoning)

Three words are given. There is a relationship between the first and second, and a similar one exists between the third and one of the answer choices.

  • Example: Day : Night = Light : ? → Darkness (opposites)
  • Cow : Barn = Dog : ? → Kennel (b) (animal's dwelling)
  • Lake : Boat = Sky : ? → Helicopter (d) (vehicle in the medium)

2. Number Sequences

Complete a number sequence by finding the next integer, using basic arithmetic.

  • Example: 1, 4, 8, 3, 6, 12, 7, ? → 10 (pattern: +3, ×2, −5 repeating: 7+3=10)
  • 8, 24, 12, 36, 18, 54, 27, ? → 81 (pattern: ×3, ÷2 alternating: 27×3=81)

3. Logic Tasks

Derive valid conclusions from two statements. Tick all valid conclusions.

Statement 1: At least one Amiv is a Plosa

Statement 2: All Plosa are Urov

Valid conclusions:

  • b) At least one Plosa is Urov (follows directly from Statement 2)
  • d) At least one Amiv is Urov (chain: Amiv → Plosa → Urov)

Note: c) "All Amiv are Plosa" does not contradict the statements but cannot be logically derived from them, and is therefore a false conclusion.

4. Spatial Reasoning

A cube is rotated/flipped in multiple steps. After each step, indicate which symbol is visible on the front face.

Tip: Sketch the cube after each step. The sketch is not graded but helps enormously.

5. Matrices

Figures arranged in a grid according to a system — construct the missing figure. The answer consists of individual elements (shape, pattern, position, direction) marked separately on the answer sheet.

Tip: Complex patterns are always composed of simple patterns. Analyse rows and columns separately.

Preparation

  • Use the literature indicated on the website for preparing the subject section: tuwien.at/studium/aufnahme-rp
  • Work through the practice examples in this knowledge base and in the Quizzes
  • Always read questions carefully before answering
  • For text comprehension: check precisely whether a statement can be reconciled with the information in the text
  • For spatial reasoning and matrices: sketch your answer in the provided drawing field
